The kiln cooled. Bricks ticked. Dr. Hale met Mr. Pell and Mrs. Adel. They stood still. She wrote times in a book. Column one was the hour. Column two was flame. Column three stayed blank.

The yard filled. A boy dropped shelves. Someone swore. Someone laughed. The crane turned. It stopped. Wind came off the canal.

She walked the racks. Upper pots were pale. Lower pots were darker. Thumbs had marked a few. She said nothing.

Mr. Pell hated the blank. A buyer was coming. She listened. She did not fill it. He would sigh. She would offer tea. Nothing would change.

Tea was strong. Mugs did not match. One mug was chipped. Mrs. Adel drank from it. Dr. Hale smiled. He ignored the ledger.

She counted pots. Forty-two sat up high. Thirty-seven sat low. Two were missing. Broken ones sat in a crate. She wrote numbers in the margin.

A train passed. Racks clicked. Nobody looked up. She thought of the buyer. She thought of the lie she would not write. Both facts stayed.

She ate bread. She ate a pear. She watched the canal. It looked like zinc. A rope dragged. A dark thread closed. She stopped counting gaps.

Afternoon meant repairs. A shelf was planed. A lid was sanded. A hinge moved. Old holes stayed. Wood kept the record.

Mrs. Adel brought post. She complained about the hill. A shop wanted twelve bowls. The order was plain. She was glad.

She answered at the bench. She wrote the date. She wrote numbers. She wrote the price. She skipped the blank column.

Evening came. The kiln opened a crack. Heat came out. Pots were shadows. Nothing had slumped. Mr. Pell stayed quiet.

She locked the gate. The crane was still. The book stayed blank. She walked home. She ignored the buses.
