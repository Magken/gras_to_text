Dear Mara,

The yard is the same yard, and I am writing to you from the step because the office chair still faces the cabinet. I turned it last winter. Nobody turned it back. That small refusal still pleases me more than it should, and I am telling you so you can laugh at me with a clear conscience.

We fired on Tuesday. The flame held a colour I do not have a name for, somewhere between straw and the inside of a loaf, and I left the third column blank upon purpose. Mr. Pell thinks a buyer will call the blank carelessness. He may be right about the buyer. He is wrong about the column. An invented colour is a lie with neat handwriting, and I will not put one in a book that already smells of ash.

Dr. Hale came by before the noon bell, which means the street is still handing out titles it did not earn. He stood with Mrs. Adel and they both looked at the kiln as if it owed them an explanation. It does not. It owes us pots that do not slump, and on Tuesday it paid that debt. Two were short of the Monday count. They are not in the crate of broken ones. I have looked.

The canal was the colour of zinc again. A rope dragged a dark thread across it and the thread closed. I started to count the gaps and then I stopped, because those gaps are not mine to file. You would have counted them anyway. You always did like a series more than a moral.

The shop on the hill wants twelve bowls by the first of the month, six pale and six dark. I wrote back with the date, the numbers, and the price. I did not mention the blank column. Some facts stay in the yard. If you visit, I will show you the margin, where the real numbers live, and I will pour you tea in the chipped mug if Mrs. Adel has not already claimed it as a philosophy.

Come if you can. The crane still turns when the wind comes off the water, and it still lifts nothing, and the nothing is starting to look like a plan.

Yours,
Helen
